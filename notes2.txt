stuff to think about from looking through my local OpenTESArena source

still a top down dungeon crawler in the browser, with shapes for the player and enemies.
these are possibilities for later, not me saying everything here has to be in the game.
mostly looking at what makes a dungeon interesting even if everything is circles and rectangles.

source paths below start inside https://github.com/afritz1/OpenTESArena/tree/main/OpenTESArena/src
the ideas for my game are adaptations, not claims that Arena does every detail the same way.


lighting around the player
OpenTESArena has a player light that follows the camera position, with a start and end radius.
it also has lights associated with other objects in the world.
for mine that could mean a clear area around the circle guy and a fade into darkness further out.
later a room could have a stationary light, so it isnt always just the player lighting everything.
walls blocking that light is still something i have to figure out separately. finding player light
in this source doesnt mean it already gives me the top down wall shadows i want.
look at: Rendering/RenderLightManager.cpp

enemies having a few things they can be doing
the enemy logic has idle, moving toward the player, and attacking the player states.
it uses distance to decide when to start following and when to stop, with different distances for
starting and stopping so an enemy doesnt keep changing its mind right at the edge.
for mine a shape could sit in a room, approach when im close, and stop to attack when it reaches me.
colors could show what its doing.
the part i read uses distance for detection, so checking whether a wall blocks its view would be
another thing to consider for mine. following me also doesnt automatically mean finding a route
around walls, so start with an enemy in one open room.
look at: Entities/EntityChunkManager.cpp, updateEnemyBehaviorStates

attacks having timing
the enemies wait between swings and only attempt a hit once during a swing, partway through it.
for mine i could have the enemy change color briefly before it hits, giving me time to get away.
same idea for the player, an attack should be an action with a beginning and end instead of damage
happening every frame two shapes touch. start with one short range attack before more weapons.
look at: Entities/EntityChunkManager.cpp, Combat/CombatLogic.cpp

making hits readable with shapes
the combat code changes health and creates hit effects and sounds. it also has bow and spell projectiles.
my version could flash a shape when it takes damage and have a small health display.
a projectile could just be another small moving shape later
Arena also has random damage and hit chance calculations, but i can start with a hit doing a fixed
amount of damage when it connects, then decide if randomness actually makes mine more fun.
look at: Combat/CombatLogic.cpp

doors and keys giving a reason to explore
the map logic handles opening doors and unlocking them with keys, lockpicking, or bashing.
there is also a separate key inventory on the player.
for mine just a closed door and one matching key somewhere else could be enough to start.
could use a colored shape for the key and mark the matching door, with a symbol too so it isnt
only color telling me which is which. lockpicking and bashing can wait.
an open door would eventually need to let both the player and their light through.
look at: World/MapLogic.cpp, handleDoorOpen; Player/Player.cpp, key inventory methods

rooms that do something when i enter
the map has triggers for sounds and text, including text that only displays once.
mine could have a room entrance show a short message or play a sound, giving the dungeon some
character without needing characters to talk to or detailed artwork.
could also adapt that idea to a trap or encounter, but thats my extension of the trigger idea.
need to remember when something has already happened so it doesnt repeat every frame im there.
look at: World/MapLogic.cpp, handleTriggersInVoxel

using my seed for actual dungeon pieces
the dungeon generation selects existing blocks with random numbers, copies their layout, brings
along locks and triggers, and places transitions between floors.
that makes me think i can build a few room and hallway pieces i like first, then have my seed
choose between those instead of trying to generate every single wall with no plan.
Math/Random.cpp has the same Arena multiplier i adapted for Seed.js.
same seed and same sequence of generation choices should give me the same layout for testing.
would still need to check that rooms connect, doorways are wide enough, and the exit is reachable.
if there is a locked door, its key needs to be reachable without already going through that door.
look at: World/MapGeneration.cpp, dungeon block generation; Math/Random.cpp

having somewhere to get to
the dungeon generation places level up and level down transitions.
mine could start with an exit shape in another room, then later stairs to another floor.
one small goal could be find the key, get past an enemy, unlock the exit, finish the dungeon.
if i let the player return to a floor, i would have to decide what stays changed, like doors being
open and enemies already defeated. thats a separate decision from generating the layout again.
look at: World/MapGeneration.cpp, levelUpBlock and levelDownBlock

floor types changing how a room feels
the player has swimming movement and the automap distinguishes ordinary floor, water, lava and pits.
for mine a blue floor area could slow movement and a dangerous area could hurt if i stay in it.
that could make choosing a path interesting even in a room with very few walls.
start with one floor effect, and make sure enemies have a sensible rule for that floor too.
look at: Player/Player.cpp; Interface/AutomapUiMVC.cpp, getPixelColor

small inventory instead of a whole equipment system
the inventory has item slots, stacks and weight, and item definitions include things like weapons,
gold and consumables. mine could just keep track of a key and one healing item at first.
something useful off the direct path gives me a reason to explore the other room.
lots of equipment slots and carrying weight are things to consider much later if they fit.
look at: Items/ItemInventory.h; Items/ItemDefinition.h

a map for when the dungeon gets bigger
the automap draws different colors for floor, walls, doors and hazards, with a player direction marker.
mine is already top down, but once darkness hides rooms a separate map could still be useful.
my version could remember rooms ive explored without showing enemies i cant currently see.
that remembering explored rooms is an idea for mine, not something verified in the automap code.
probably no need for this until there is enough dungeon to actually get lost in.
look at: Interface/AutomapUiMVC.cpp

doors actually swinging open
the door animation tracks closed, opening, open and closing, and how far open the door is.
the door helpers turn that progress into roughly a quarter turn for a swinging door.
that fits with rotating my walls. a thin rectangle could rotate around one end over time instead
of disappearing when i open it. would need to decide when it stops blocking movement and light.
look at: Voxels/VoxelDoorAnimationInstance.cpp; Voxels/VoxelDoorUtils.cpp

loot that stays taken
the interface opens inventories on containers and defeated enemies. an empty container can be
removed, while empty corpses are kept. creature gold has a separate first interaction step.
mine could leave a little shape behind when an enemy dies, with a pickup i can collect once.
need to remember what is left so coming back doesnt generate a new reward every time.
the source even has a note that creature gold should be decided when the creature is created.
look at: Interface/GameWorldUiMVC.cpp, onContainerInventoryOpened and onEnemyCorpseInteractedFirstTime

somewhere to recover
the player has rest healing, and the entity manager has nearby-enemy checks using a safe sleep
distance, ignoring enemies that are dying or dead.
my version could allow resting to heal when enemies arent nearby, or have one safe room to recover in.
havent traced the complete camping flow, this is an idea from those pieces.
look at: Player/Player.cpp, applyRestHealing; Entities/EntityChunkManager.cpp, anyEnemiesNearby

knowing what a thing is when i point at it
the interface has an inspect action that can show a living enemy's name.
once cursor tracking is in my engine, hovering a shape could show its name or a short description.
could help give shapes an identity, like a rat versus a dungeon guard.
hovering is my adaptation, the source's inspect action isnt a browser hover system.
look at: Interface/GameWorldUiMVC.cpp, onEnemyAliveInspected

magic to look into
https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Arena:Magic

ideas from the UESP Known Spellbook
the versions for my game below are simplified ideas, not the original spell formulas.
i definitely wont get to making all too many spells, but here is some ideas i can maybe try

light being something i can change
Light is centered on the caster, which matches the player centered lighting i already want.
Wanderlight is a moving globe of light, so later i could send a little glowing shape down a hallway
to see ahead. it would need its own position and lifetime, and my wall visibility rules should
still apply. my normal player light could stay on, with a spell temporarily extending its reach.

a shield i can actually see
Shield creates a shield with a certain amount of points. mine could show a ring around the player
that takes damage before health does, then disappears when its used up.
Force Wall is also a shield spell in this list, despite the name. Earth Wall is the one making walls.
this feels like a readable effect even when the player is just a circle.

different ways of delivering the same attack
the spellbook distinguishes touching one target, hitting one at range, an explosion at range,
and an area centered on the caster.
for mine Fire Dart could inspire one small projectile, then a Fireball style attack could burst
into a circle on impact. a Fire Storm style attack would instead happen around the player.
dont need twenty damage formulas to make those feel different, placement and range already matter.
start with the single projectile before adding explosions.

effects that last for a while
Wildfire repeats damage, Troll's Blood repeats healing, and Medusa's Gaze can paralyze a target.
mine could eventually have one timed effect with a visible indicator, like a burning enemy changing
color or a stunned enemy briefly stopping. decide what happens if the same effect is applied twice.
Heal is the simpler place to start, just restoring some health without going above the maximum.

magic changing a route
Open can unlock something, Wizard Lock can lock a door, Earth Wall creates walls, and Passwall
destroys walls. these could make the dungeon about choosing routes as well as fighting.
for mine start with a spell that opens one marked door or breaks one specially marked wall.
being able to remove every wall would make my key and exit layout mean something very different.
if a wall changes, collision and visibility would both need to use the changed layout.
the supplied notes say Arena's created and destroyed walls reset when leaving that dungeon level,
so i would need to choose whether mine remembers changes or resets them too.

avoiding a fight
Invisibility suggests a spell that changes whether an enemy notices the player.
making my circle transparent is just the presentation, the enemy behavior would have to respect
the effect too. i would need to decide whether attacking ends it and what an already chasing enemy does.
those are rules for my adaptation, not claims about exactly how Arena's invisibility works.

keeping magic small
Arena has costs, level scaling, saving throws and lots of overlapping spell variants.
for mine maybe a projectile, a shield and a light boost would be enough to experiment with first,
one at a time. a fixed cost and duration are easier to judge than copying every original formula.
wall changing spells are a later experiment once ordinary walls and visibility work reliably.
